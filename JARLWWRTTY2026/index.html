New sponsor, same contest

This event ran as the JARTS WW RTTY Contest through 2025 and is JARL-sponsored from 2026. Same contest, same weekend, same rules — every historical log on this page says CONTEST: JARTS-WW-RTTY in its Cabrillo header, and that continuity is what makes four years of our own data usable for a first JARL running.

That the scoring is genuinely unchanged is tested, not assumed: scoring the 2022–2025 logs under the 2026 JARL rules reproduces N1MM's own claimed score to within 0.5% in the best year and 1.7% in the worst. Had the rules moved with the sponsor, that check would have failed.

How the score is built

RTTY (Baudot) on 3.5/7/14/21/28 MHz, each station workable once per band. The exchange is RST plus your age — average age for multi-op, and 01, 00 or 99 are all acceptable.

  • Points: 3 to a different continent, 2 to the same one — including the same entity. There is no domestic discount, so a W-to-W contact is worth 2, not the 1 that CQ WW pays.
  • Multipliers, per band: every DXCC entity excluding JA/W/VE/VK mainland, plus every call area of JA/W/VE/VK by prefix digit. So W0–W9, VE1–9, JA1–0 and the VK areas are all multipliers, and a domestic run is a multiplier hunt.
  • Score = total points × total multipliers. The rule reads "(Total QSO points for each band) x (Total multipliers … in each band)", which is easy to misread as a per-band product. It is not: "per band" governs the counting — a station counts once on each band, an entity counts once on each band — and then the grand totals multiply. Reading it the other way came out 4.46× too small against the logs, which is how it was settled.

A multiplier is worth about four QSOs — and that changes the bands

In an average year this station logs about 4,272 points and 362 multipliers, so one more multiplier adds roughly 4,272 points of score while one more QSO adds about 1,000. A multiplier is worth 4.3 QSOs.

Because score is a product, a QSO's real value is M·(its points) + P·(its chance of being a new multiplier). Run that over four years of our own logs and the bands separate sharply:

This is the exact inverse of Makrothen two weeks ago, where 80m was marginal because distance was the score and 80m's reach from FN32 is short. Here reach barely matters and novelty does.

Where the multipliers come from

Four years pooled: 1,042 DXCC entity multipliers (72%) against 406 JA/W/VE/VK call-area multipliers (28%). The call areas are a smaller pool — at most ten W, nine VE, ten JA and a handful of VK per band — but they are also the ones most reliably available, since the domestic path is open when nothing else is.

How this was built

  • An 83-day archive of spots to/from FN32 ± 1 grid square (111M spots, 22 Jul – 13 Oct 2026), reduced to opening and closing edges per CQ zone and band as offsets from the terminator that bounds each path, then resolved against the contest weekend's own sunrise and sunset.
  • This station's own four logs, 2022–2025 — 6,185 deduplicated QSOs, with entity and call area resolved through the project's own callsign_lookup rather than hand-rolled prefix parsing.

One limitation, stated plainly. The band plan groups openings by CQ zone, but JARL's multipliers are DXCC entities and call areas, which are finer — zone 14 alone holds a dozen entities. The chart tells you when a region opens; how many multipliers live there is a separate question it does not answer. Entity-level grouping would need a different sweep than the covering indexes support, and four days before the contest was not the moment to attempt it.

Caveat, stated on the chart: the offsets come from 22 Jul – 13 Oct 2026; the window drifts one way through the season, 14 h 48 m of daylight down to 11 h 10 m. The target night runs 10 h 59 m, 0 h 11 m shorter than the shortest day in it. That is why the edges are measured against the terminator rather than in UTC — only the offsets carry across that gap, and the Carrington weighting already leans the sample toward the window’s recent end. This is an extrapolation rather than an interpolation.
