Reading the map

Bearing reads straight off the map (north up) and distance is radial, with dashed rings every 3000 km. In this contest the rings are the score — a QSO is worth its distance in kilometres, so a station two rings out is worth twice one at a single ring. That is the opposite of CQ WW, where the rings were dropped from these maps as decoration; here they are the point. Multiply by the band factor to read points: on 80m a 3000 km ring is 6,000 points, on 40m 4,500, elsewhere 3,000.

The faint lattice is the grid-field grid, which is what the exchange actually sends. A rolling 30-minute window slides one minute per frame, 1440 frames looping the day from 0000Z. Night is shaded for 10 October with a lighter band to the end of nautical twilight, the sun marks the subsolar point, and the auroral ovals are the nominal Kp≈2 extent. Append #f=N to a page URL to open it paused N minutes after 0000Z.

All three operating periods are on this one clock: 0000–0800Z and 1600–2400Z are Saturday, 0800–1600Z is Sunday.
