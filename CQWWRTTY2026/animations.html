Reading the map

Bearing reads straight off the map (north up); distance is radial, with dashed rings every 3000 km — range only, since CQ WW scores by continent rather than distance. The magenta outlines are the CQ zone boundaries with their zone numbers, drawn instead of the grid-field lattice: zones are the multipliers here, and grid fields are not. Numbers are painted over the activity rather than under it, so the busiest zones stay readable. A rolling 30-minute window slides one minute per frame, 1440 frames looping the day from 0000Z, the contest clock. Night is shaded for the contest date with a lighter band from sunset to the end of nautical twilight, the sun marks the subsolar point, and the auroral ovals are the nominal Kp≈2 extent — optimistic for a weekend forecast at Kp 4–5. Append #f=N to a page URL to open it paused N minutes after 0000Z.

160m is included for completeness; CQ WW RTTY is a five-band contest (80 through 10 m) and 160m is not part of it.
